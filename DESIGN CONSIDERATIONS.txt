Battlefield 2 has a very limited lighting system so whenever I hit these limits, I had to make design decisions.

I build in several mechanisms to compensate missing secondary light reflections. No bright surface would ever brighten any shadow.

1. Although there is hemisphere lighting i wanted ambient hue to affect foliage under specific conditions for a more coherent scene.
The idea is that if sunlight is strong, more ambient coloring will be reflected by the foliage. This had to be limited to foliage only because else the hand-picked hue would dominate the whole scene and would feel off.

2. To keep shadows from blacking out around noon a gate shifts light budget from direct towards indirect light while preserving hue.
You can imagine the light budget as a given amount of light that can exist in the scene and is only shifted to one side or the other.
A sun-intensity driven gate solve this problem better than a static parameter floor.

3 At late afternoon I had the problem that shadows would stay cool while the sun would get increasingly warm. 
At these elevations I added an influencing factor, where static shadows would rise in color temperature for a limited range.

4. For all terms present increasing overcast means linearly shifting direct light to indirect light. This caused the problem that statics would
lose their definition and have an overly flat appearance. Static lighting does not differentiate between thin lamp posts and large structures.
The solution is retaining some amount of direct light for static objects only. 

6. There is a early morning-specific hue switch for direct light that I decided to introduce to suppress overly orange morning scenes. 
For a brief amount of time terrain, bundledmesh, and statics sun light would be sky-colored blue, while I preserved the orange for the foliage sun, and specular colors.
This looks incredibly cool without drenching the whole morning landscape in orange.
